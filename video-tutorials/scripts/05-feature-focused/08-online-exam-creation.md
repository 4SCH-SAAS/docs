**Visual Cues:**

  - Recycling icon (reusable exams)
  - Lock icon (security and integrity)
- Show before/after comparison:
  - Traditional: hours of manual grading
  - Online: instant automated grading

**Voiceover Script:**
"Online exams offer numerous benefits for teachers and students.

First, they save time dramatically. Automated grading means no more late-night scoring sessions. Instant feedback helps students understand what they got wrong immediately, enhancing learning.

Second, you get rich analytics. The system shows you which questions students struggled with, which topics need reteaching, and which students need extra support. This data-driven approach improves instruction.

Third, online exams are accessible. You can add accommodations like extended time, large text, or text-to-speech for students who need them—all configured centrally without creating separate versions.

Fourth, exams are reusable. Create an exam once and use it year after year. Build a question bank over time, and create new exams efficiently by selecting questions from your library.

Finally, online exams maintain academic integrity. You can randomize question order, randomize answer choices, and set time limits. You can also monitor student activity during exams to detect cheating.

Students benefit too. They receive instant feedback, can review their answers, and develop computer skills they'll need in college and careers."

### Step 2: Configuring Exam Details (2:15 - 3:15)

**Visual Cues:**
- Screen recording: Exam creation form with sections:
  - Exam title field
  - Description field
  - Subject/topic selection
  - Exam type dropdown (quiz, midterm, final, diagnostic)
  - Start date/time picker
  - End date/time picker
  - Time limit toggle and input
  - Instructions/rules text area
- Show preview updating as details are entered
- Display security settings section:
  - Randomize questions toggle
  - Randomize answers toggle
  - Shuffle questions checkbox

**Voiceover Script:**
"Start by entering your exam details. Give your exam a clear title like 'Chapter 5 Midterm' or 'Fall Semester Final Exam.'

Add a description explaining the purpose of the exam. This helps students understand what they're being assessed on.

Select the subject or topic. This helps organize your exams and connect them to your curriculum.

Choose the exam type. Is this a quick quiz to check understanding, a chapter exam, a midterm, or a final? This categorization helps you track different types of assessments.

Set your start and end dates. Students can take the exam anytime between these dates. You might open an exam Monday morning and close it Friday afternoon, giving students flexibility.

Configure the time limit. If this is a timed exam, set how long students have. For example, a 50-minute class period might have a 45-minute exam. If there's no time limit, toggle that off—useful for take-home exams or assignments completed over several days.

Add instructions that students see before starting. Be clear: 'You have 45 minutes to complete this exam. Do not discuss answers with other students. Use only your textbook and class notes.'

Under security settings, enable question randomization so each student sees questions in a different order. Enable answer randomization so correct answer positions vary. This helps prevent cheating by making it harder for students to share answers.

Once you've configured these details, click 'Next' or 'Add Questions' to move to the question selection step."

**Visual Cues:**
- Screen recording: Question addition interface with options:
  1. Create new question:
     - Question type dropdown (multiple choice, short answer, essay, true/false, matching)
     - Question text editor
     - Answer options
     - Correct answer selection
     - Points value
  2. Add from question bank:
     - Search question bank
     - Browse by category
     - Select questions (checkboxes)
     - Add to exam
  3. Import questions:
     - Upload CSV or file
     - Map fields
     - Review imported questions
- Show question preview as each is added
- Display exam outline showing all questions

**Voiceover Script:**
"Now add questions to your exam. The system offers three approaches.

First, create new questions right here. Select your question type from the dropdown: multiple choice, short answer, essay, true/false, matching, or others depending on your system.

For multiple choice, write your question, then add answer options. For example, question: 'What is the capital of France?' Options: Paris, London, Berlin, Madrid. Select the correct answer—Paris.

Assign points to each question. A simple quiz might be 1 point per question. A midterm might have 2-5 point questions depending on complexity.

Write an explanation or hint students see after answering. This supports learning even after the exam.

For essay questions, you'll typically grade manually. The system collects student responses but you score them yourself.

Second, use your question bank. If you've been building reusable questions, search or browse your library. Select the questions you want to include. This is much faster than creating new questions every time.

Third, import questions from a file. If you have a CSV, Excel spreadsheet, or other format with existing questions, the system can import them. Map the columns—question text, answer options, correct answer, points—and the system loads them all at once.

As you add questions, you'll see your exam outline showing all included questions, their point values, and the total possible points. Make sure you have the right mix and difficulty of questions."

### Step 4: Setting Grading and Review Options (4:30 - 5:15)

**Visual Cues:**
- Screen recording: Grading configuration section with options:
  - Grading scale selection (points, percentage, letter grade)
  - Passing score threshold
  - Curve or extra credit options
  - Review options:
    - Show answers immediately after submit
    - Show answers after deadline passes
    - Show score but not answers
    - Show nothing until teacher releases results
  - Feedback display:
    - Show correct answer
    - Show explanation
    - Show student's answer vs. correct answer
- Show preview of student view with different review settings

**Voiceover Script:**
"Configure how the exam will be graded and reviewed. Select your grading scale. Most systems use points or percentages. For example, an exam worth 100 points or calculated as a percentage of total points.

Set the passing score or cut-off for different grades. For a typical 100-point scale, you might set A as 90-100, B as 80-89, etc.

Decide when students see results. You have several options:

Show answers immediately after submission. Students see their score and correct answers right away. This is great for learning and allows students to understand what they got wrong while it's fresh.

Show answers only after the deadline passes. This lets all students complete the exam before anyone sees answers, preventing cheating in a self-paced format.

Show score but not answers until you review. This gives you time to review the results before students see them.

Show nothing until you manually release results. This maintains suspense and gives you time to discuss exam results in class before students see their scores.

For each approach, configure what students see: their score, the correct answer, the explanation you provided, or a comparison of their answer versus the correct answer.

Most teachers choose to show answers immediately after submission for quizzes, allowing instant feedback. For major exams like midterms, they might show nothing until they've reviewed and discussed results in class."

**Production Notes:**
- Show grading configuration clearly
- Display all review options
- Show preview of each student view option
- Highlight differences between approaches
- Include examples for each setting
- Use callout for best practices
- Display grading scale visually

As students take the exam, you can monitor progress in real-time. The teacher dashboard shows which students have started, who's still working, and who's completed.

Once students submit, the system automatically grades objective questions like multiple choice. Short answer and essay questions are tagged for your manual review.

The results dashboard gives you powerful insights. See the average score, view a distribution of scores to understand how your class performed overall, and analyze each question. 'Only 30 percent of students got question 7 correct? That topic needs reteaching.'

**Visual Cues:**

- Icons for each practice:
  - Lightbulb (clear questions)
  - People (fairness)
  - Graph (analytics)

**Voiceover Script:**
"Best practices for online exams:

One: Write clear questions. Ambiguous questions frustrate students and don't assess learning accurately.

Two: Build question banks over time. Reuse questions you've created and tested. As you teach the same course year after year, your question bank grows, making exam creation faster.

Three: Use exam data to improve teaching. Don't just post grades—analyze which topics students struggled with and adjust your instruction.

Four: Provide feedback. Let students see explanations so they learn from mistakes, even after the exam ends."
