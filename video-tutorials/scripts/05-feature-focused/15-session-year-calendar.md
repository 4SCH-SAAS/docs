### Understanding Session Years (0:45 - 1:45)

**[VISUAL]**
- Animation showing a 12-month calendar

**[VOICEOVER]:**  
"First, let's clarify what a session year is. A session year, or academic year, represents one complete cycle of your school's operations - typically running from September through August in Nigerian schools, though some run from April to March.

For example, Session 2025-2026 might run from September 1, 2025 to August 31, 2026.

Why does this matter? Because every single piece of data in your school - student records, exam results, fee payments, attendance tracking - is organized by session year. When you transition to a new academic year, students get promoted, and their records move to the new session.

Think of your session year as a container. Everything that happens during that 12-month period stays together. When you need historical data from a previous year, you simply filter by that session year and all the information is there.

In Nigeria, most secondary schools follow the September-to-August academic calendar, though some primary schools or international schools might use different patterns. Whatever your school uses, you'll set that up here."

**[VISUAL]**
- Animation showing a calendar year divided into sections
- Highlight semester/term divisions

**[VOICEOVER]:**  
"Now that you have your session year created, you might want to divide it into semesters or terms. This is optional but very common in Nigerian schools.

Many secondary schools run on a **two-semester system**:
- Semester 1: September to December/January
- Semester 2: January/February to August

Some schools, especially primary schools, use a **three-term system**:
- Term 1: September to December
- Term 2: January to April
- Term 3: May to August

Why use semesters? Several reasons: You conduct mid-year exams at the end of Semester 1 and final exams at the end of Semester 2. You issue report cards per semester. Some schools collect fees on a per-semester basis. Having semesters defined helps organize all of this.

If your school doesn't use distinct semesters - if you do continuous assessment throughout the year - you can skip this step. But if you run structured exams, I recommend setting up semesters."

### Best Practices (7:45 - 8:45)

**[VISUAL]**
- Checklist animation appearing
- Timeline showing "Before New Session", "During Session", "End of Session"
- Calendar showing planning ahead

**[VOICEOVER]:**  
"Let me share best practices for managing your academic calendar effectively.

**Start Planning Early**  
Create your new session year at least 2-3 months before it begins. Why? Because other configurations depend on it:
- Fee structures for the new year
- Timetables for classes
- Subject assignments to teachers
- Exam schedules

If you wait until August to set up the September session, you'll be scrambling at the last minute.

**Use Consistent Naming**  
Always use the format YYYY-YYYY for your session years. Don't mix "2025-26" with "2025-2026" - consistency matters for reporting and parent communication. Choose your format and stick with it.

**Account for Exam and Holiday Periods**  
When setting semester dates, ensure you have adequate time after semester ends for exams and result publication. For Semester 1 ending January 31, you need time in February for exams before Semester 2 starts. Don't overlap.

**Coordinate Holidays with the Nigerian Calendar**  
Check the official Nigerian Public Service notice for holiday dates each year. Some holidays are gazetted by the federal government. Align your school closures with national holidays to avoid confusion.

**Communicate the Calendar Early**  
Once finalized, share your academic calendar with parents, teachers, and staff. Many schools publish it in:
- Parent handbook
- Staff meetings
- Website
- WhatsApp parent groups
- Student assembly announcements

**Include Nigerian Context**  
When adding holidays, include national holidays AND Islamic holidays (Eid-el-Fitr, Eid-el-Kabir) as applicable to your school's diversity. This shows respect for all cultures in your student body.

**Plan Student Promotion Timing**  
At the end of your session year, you need time to promote students to the next class in the new session. Plan this 2-3 weeks before session ends, so new enrollments are ready on the first day of school.

**Backup and Archive Old Sessions**  
As new sessions are created, the old ones become archived. They remain in the system for historical reference, but you can't edit them anymore. This protects historical data integrity."
